#1 Private boat tour to Capri 8.8

Best for: couples, families and groups of 4 to 10

A private day follows the same basic route as the shared tours, but the skipper adapts it to you: longer swims at the Faraglioni, lunch at a beach club in Marina Piccola or Anacapri, an early start to beat the queue at the Blue Grotto. Departures are possible from Positano, Amalfi, Praiano or Sorrento.

Price depends mostly on the boat. A traditional wooden gozzo with a sun canopy is the classic choice, a speedboat is faster and better from Amalfi, a yacht costs considerably more. Split between six or eight people, a gozzo is often not much more per head than a shared tour.

Ask your skipper about lunch near Bagni di Tiberio, west of Marina Grande, where you swim next to the remains of a Roman villa.

What works

  • Completely flexible itinerary
  • Best for families, honeymoons and groups
  • Skipper doubles as a local guide

Before you book

  • Fuel and port fees may be extra
  • Clarify what drinks and food are included
  • Book early for July and August

From: Positano, Amalfi, Sorrento · 6–8 h · €900–2,200 per boat

#2 Capri boat tour from Positano (shared) 8.6

Best for: first-time visitors staying in Positano

Boats leave from the pier on Positano's main beach in the morning, pass the Li Galli islands and cross to Capri past Punta Campanella. Most of the morning is a slow lap of the island: the Faraglioni rocks and the arch, the green and white grottoes, the Punta Carena lighthouse, and a stop outside the Blue Grotto if it is open. You then get two to four hours ashore at Marina Grande to ride the funicular up to the Piazzetta or have lunch, before sailing back in the late afternoon.

Small-group boats (roughly 10 to 15 people) are noticeably more pleasant than large ones. Drinks and snorkelling gear are often included, lunch on Capri usually is not.

A small detail people miss: the three Li Galli islands were bought by the dancer Léonide Massine in the 1920s and later by Rudolf Nureyev. They are still private, so you see them from the boat only.

What works

  • Covers almost every Capri highlight in one day
  • Positano is close enough to Capri for an easy crossing
  • Several swim stops in clear water
  • Good value per person

Before you book

  • Blue Grotto entry is extra and depends on the sea
  • Free time on Capri is busy in July and August
  • Large boats mean less flexibility

From: Positano · 7–8 h · €95–170 per person

#3 Private Amalfi Coast boat tour (half or full day) 8.5

Best for: families and anyone who prefers swimming to sightseeing

Without the crossing to Capri, a private boat can spend its time on the coast itself: the tiny beach of Atrani, the Emerald Grotto at Conca dei Marini, the Furore fjord and its bridge, the coves of Praiano, Positano from the sea and the Li Galli islands. Many people book three or four hours, ending with a swim before sunset.

It is the calmest boat option and the easiest with young children, because you can stop and turn back whenever you like.

The Emerald Grotto was found by a local fisherman in 1932, and there is a ceramic nativity scene on the seabed inside. The bridge over the Furore fjord is about 30 metres high and is used for diving competitions in summer.

What works

  • Calm water and short distances
  • Flexible length, from 3 hours
  • Ideal for swimming and young children

Before you book

  • Emerald Grotto visit has its own ticket
  • Shorter tours cover only part of the coast
  • Check whether fuel is included

From: Positano, Amalfi · 3–8 h · €450–1,500 per boat

#4 Positano sunset boat tour 8.4

Best for: couples and anyone with only one evening

Short evening cruises leave Positano in the late afternoon, sail toward the Li Galli islands for a swim and return as the sun drops behind the coast, so you see Positano light up from the water. Most include a glass of prosecco or limoncello and snacks.

It is the best boat option if you only have an evening, and an easy first time on a boat for children or anyone unsure about seasickness.

In early summer the sun goes down behind the ridge toward Sorrento rather than into the sea. The real show is the light turning the houses pink and orange.

What works

  • Short and easy to fit in
  • Best light of the day
  • Good introduction for children

Before you book

  • Exact sunset view depends on the season
  • Shared versions can feel crowded
  • Not a sightseeing tour

From: Positano · 2–3 h · €50–95 per person

#5 Capri boat tour from Sorrento (shared) 8.4

Best for: travellers based in Sorrento or arriving from Naples

Sorrento is the closest base to Capri, so the crossing from Marina Piccola past the Sorrento peninsula takes well under an hour. That leaves more of the day for the island lap, the swim stops and free time ashore. Many boats also pass the Bay of Ieranto and the Punta Campanella marine reserve.

Because Sorrento has more tour operators, the choice is wider and prices are a little lower. Pick a small group and check whether the hotel pickup or the meeting point at the harbour is included.

The Bay of Ieranto, just before Punta Campanella, is protected and looked after by the FAI, the Italian National Trust. It is one of the clearest swims in the area when the boat stops there.

What works

  • Shortest crossing, more time on Capri
  • Widest choice of operators
  • Often the lowest price per person

Before you book

  • Does not show the Amalfi Coast itself
  • Busy harbour in high season
  • Check pickup arrangements

From: Sorrento · 7–8 h · €80–150 per person

#6 Capri island boat tour & Blue Grotto (from Capri) 8.4

Best for: day trippers already on Capri

If you reach Capri by ferry, the cheapest way to see it from the sea is a round-the-island trip from Marina Grande. In one to two hours you pass the Blue Grotto, the Punta Carena lighthouse, the coves of the south coast and the Faraglioni. Some operators let you get off at the Blue Grotto to join the queue for the rowing boats.

Comfort varies a lot: shared boats can be crowded, so small-group or private versions are worth the extra in summer.

The Blue Grotto was known to the Romans, then mostly avoided by locals for centuries. It became famous after the German writer August Kopisch wrote about it in 1826.

What works

  • Cheapest way to see Capri by sea
  • Short, easy to combine with a day on the island
  • Option to stop at the Blue Grotto

Before you book

  • You need to reach Capri first, by ferry
  • Few or no swim stops
  • Busy boats at midday in summer

From: Capri (Marina Grande) · 1–2 h · €20–45 per person

#7 Capri boat tour from Amalfi (shared) 8.1

Best for: travellers based in Amalfi, Ravello or Minori

From Amalfi the boat first runs west along the coast past Conca dei Marini, Praiano and Positano, so you see the coast from the water before even reaching Capri. Expect about an hour and a half of sailing each way. On Capri the programme is similar to tours from Positano: a lap of the island with swim stops, the Blue Grotto if open, and free time ashore.

It is the right choice if you are staying in Amalfi, Atrani, Ravello or Minori and want Capri without first travelling to Positano. If you are flexible, starting from Positano shortens the day.

On the way west, look up at Praiano: the majolica dome of San Gennaro is one of the brightest things on the coast in morning light.

What works

  • Sees the whole coastline from the sea
  • No need to travel to Positano first
  • Usually includes swim stops on both legs

Before you book

  • Longest day of all the shared tours
  • More time sailing, slightly less on Capri
  • Afternoon return can be choppy

From: Amalfi · 8–9 h · €100–180 per person

#8 Amalfi Coast boat tour from Sorrento (Positano & Amalfi) 8.0

Best for: Sorrento guests who want to see Positano and Amalfi

The boat rounds Punta Campanella and follows the coast east past Nerano and the Li Galli islands to Positano, where you usually get time ashore, then continues past Praiano and the Furore fjord to Amalfi for lunch and a visit to the cathedral. The return follows the same route with a swim stop.

It replaces the famous but crowded coast road with a far more relaxing day, and it is the best way to see Positano's cliffside houses from the angle in every photo.

From the sea you get the full view of Positano's church of Santa Maria Assunta and its green and yellow tiled dome, which is hard to see properly from the lanes.

What works

  • Avoids the traffic on the coast road
  • Time ashore in Positano and Amalfi
  • Postcard views from the water

Before you book

  • Long sailing time if the sea is choppy
  • Does not include Capri
  • Short stops if the boat runs late

From: Sorrento · 7–8 h · €90–160 per person

If you just want an answer

  • First time here, normal budget: a shared Capri trip from wherever you are staying.
  • Kids, a group of six or more, or a special occasion: a private boat. Capri if you have the whole day, the coast if you do not.
  • Only one free evening: the sunset trip out of Positano.
  • Already on Capri for the day: the round-the-island boat from Marina Grande, then lunch.

Frequently asked questions

Are these rankings based on reviews?
No. They are editorial scores built on five published criteria. Customer ratings appear separately in the GetYourGuide listings, which come from verified bookings.
Is Capri or the Amalfi Coast better by boat?
Capri has the more famous sights (Faraglioni, Blue Grotto) and lets you go ashore. The coast route is calmer, shorter and better for swimming in quiet coves. With one day, most people choose Capri.
Which tour is best for a honeymoon?
A private boat, either a full day to Capri or a half day along the coast ending at sunset off Positano.
