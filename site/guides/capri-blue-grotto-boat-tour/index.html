Capri and the Blue Grotto by boat

The Blue Grotto is the most famous sight on Capri and the most misunderstood.

Updated November 2026 · By the Amalfi Coast Boat Guide team

No boat tour can enter the Blue Grotto itself. Tours stop outside and you transfer to a small rowing boat, paying a separate fee on the spot. It closes when the sea is rough, so treat it as a bonus, not the reason for the trip.

How entry works

The entrance is a hole in the rock about a metre high. Visitors move from their tour boat into small rowing boats holding a few people, lie back as the rower pulls through on a chain, and spend a few minutes inside the glowing blue cave. The fee for the rowing boat and the entry is paid there, in recent seasons around €20 per person. Bring cash and check the current price.

When it closes

Even a moderate swell makes the entrance unsafe, so the grotto closes regularly, sometimes for days in spring and autumn. It is generally open from morning to late afternoon in calm conditions. Early in the day queues are shorter and the sea is usually calmer.

Queues

In July and August the wait at sea can exceed an hour. Private boats can time it better; shared tours decide on the day whether waiting is worth it.

Which tour to choose

  • Staying on the coast: a Capri boat tour from Positano, Amalfi or Sorrento.
  • Already on Capri: the island tour from Marina Grande.
  • Want the best chance: a private boat with an early start.

Beyond the Blue Grotto

The Faraglioni rocks, the arch you can sail through, the green and white grottoes and the coves near Punta Carena are often more memorable than the Blue Grotto itself, and they are on every island lap.

Frequently asked questions

Can you swim in the Blue Grotto?
No. Swimming inside is not allowed during opening hours.
Is the Blue Grotto worth it?
In calm weather and without a long queue, yes: the light is extraordinary. With a long wait, many visitors prefer more swim time elsewhere.
Can children enter the Blue Grotto?
Yes, children travel in the rowing boats with their parents.
