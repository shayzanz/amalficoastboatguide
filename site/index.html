Two routes, many listings

Search for a boat tour here and you get hundreds of results. Strip away the photos and almost all of them follow one of two routes.

The first goes west to Capri. You pass Li Galli, the three small islands Nureyev once owned, round Punta Campanella and spend the morning circling Capri: the Faraglioni (the boat usually goes through the arch in the middle one), the Green and White grottoes, the lighthouse at Punta Carena and, if the sea allows, the Blue Grotto. Then a few hours ashore at Marina Grande.

The second stays on the coast. Positano from the water, Praiano, the fjord at Furore with its bridge, the Emerald Grotto, Amalfi and tiny Atrani next door. Shorter, calmer, more swimming.

After that it comes down to who is on board and how long you have. Two people on a budget are fine on a shared boat. A family of five, or anyone celebrating something, should at least get a price for a private gozzo. It is often closer than people expect.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best boat tour on the Amalfi Coast?
For most first-time visitors, a small-group Capri boat tour from Positano gives the best mix of sights, swim stops and price. Families and groups of six or more usually do better with a private boat. See our full ranking.
How much does a boat tour on the Amalfi Coast cost?
Shared full-day tours to Capri typically cost €80–180 per person depending on the starting town and season. Private boats start around €450 for a few hours on the coast and reach €900–2,200 per boat for a full day to Capri. Details in our price guide.
Do boat tours to Capri include the Blue Grotto?
Most stop outside the Blue Grotto when it is open, but entry is by small rowing boats and is paid separately on the spot. It closes in rough seas, so no tour can guarantee it.
